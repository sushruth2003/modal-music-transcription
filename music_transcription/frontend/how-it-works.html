How it works Modal · L4

Inside the pipeline

A recording becomes
a musical timeline.

Auto Transcribe combines beat tracking and a specialized music model. It does not search for an existing score: it listens to your recording and predicts each note.

01

Prepare

Normalize the recording

Audio is extracted from video when needed, converted to mono, and resampled to 16 kHz so every job reaches the models in the same format.

02

Find time

Detect beats and meter

Beat This estimates the beat grid, downbeats, tempo, and likely meter. This gives the transcription a musical clock instead of raw seconds alone.

03

Listen

Decode notes on a GPU

MuScriptor Large runs on an NVIDIA L4 and predicts note starts, note ends, pitch, and instrument. Optional instrument hints narrow its choices.

04

Correct time

Align notes to the beat grid

A conservative timing pass tests whether the beat grid is trustworthy, then nudges nearby onsets and note lengths onto sensible subdivisions without flattening expressive timing.

05

Deliver

Build playable artifacts

The corrected events become a piano roll and MIDI file. If requested, MuseScore turns that MIDI into a printable PDF draft.

Upload→16 kHz audio→Beat grid→GPU notes→MIDI + PDF

What to expect

A useful first draft, not a perfect score.

Clean recordings with distinct instruments work best. Dense mixes, room noise, heavy effects, very fast passages, and overlapping instruments can produce missed or extra notes.

The PDF is automatically quantized from predicted MIDI. Use the MIDI in a DAW or notation editor when you want to refine voicing, rhythm, key signatures, or engraving.

Small public beta

Built to be shareable and inexpensive.

Jobs are durable, so the result URL still works after you close the tab. Compute scales down when idle, runs one GPU job at a time, and is protected by hourly, daily, and shared monthly limits.
